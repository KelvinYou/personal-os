# Training Timing — Evidence-Based Reference

Evidence base for scheduling training time slots. Based on the latest 2024-2025 meta-analyses and RCTs.

## Core Conclusions (TL;DR)

1. **Training time of day has no significant effect on hypertrophy/strength gains** — consistency
   matters far more than time of day
2. **The real variable is "the gap between training end and sleep onset"** — not morning vs evening
   per se
3. **Evening resistance training ≠ evening endurance training** — resistance training's negative
   effect on sleep is much smaller than endurance training's
4. **Individual chronotype has a significant moderating effect** — early chronotypes are more disrupted
   by evening training

## Key Evidence

### 1. Leota et al. 2025 (Nature Communications) — largest sample to date

**Sample**: 14,689 WHOOP users × 1 year of biometric data, dose-response analysis

**Dose-response results**:

| Gap between training end and sleep | Sleep impact | Recommendation |
|---|---|---|
| **≥ 4h** | No difference from non-training days (any intensity is safe) | ✅ Optimal |
| **2h** | Light exercise -15min / high intensity -45min of sleep | ⚠️ Acceptable |
| **< 1h** | Significant disruption: delayed sleep onset / reduced duration / elevated next-day RHR, lowered HRV | ❌ Avoid |

**Mechanism**: Evening training lowers parasympathetic activity → elevated RHR + reduced HRV persisting
into the next day

### 2. Resistance vs endurance (Frontiers Physiology 2024)

Differential effects of evening training type on sleep:

| Metric | Resistance training | Endurance training |
|------|---------|---------|
| Sleep efficiency | ↓ 2-3% | ↓ 2-3% |
| **Cortisol elevation** | Lower | **Significantly higher** (p=0.038) |
| **Beta arousal (N2 sleep)** | **↓ -0.046** (actually beneficial) | ↑ +0.025 (disruptive) |
| Total sleep time | Not significant | ↓ trend |

**Key finding**: Evening **resistance training has a much smaller negative effect on sleep than endurance
training**. Heavy lifting actually reduces N2-stage beta arousal — potentially aiding sleep.

**Practical implication**: Z2 / cardio / running must be scheduled AM or on weekends — **don't put them
in the evening**.

### 3. Timing vs hypertrophy (2025 ACSM Position Stand + Sports Medicine Open)

- **Strength gains are not affected by time of day** (ACSM 2025)
- **14 meta-analyses + 178 studies**: timing has no direct effect on hypertrophy magnitude (Sports Medicine
  Open)
- **2025 systematic review (14 studies / 716 subjects)**: morning vs evening shows no significant
  difference in body composition / BMI / sleep latency / lipid profile

**Conclusion**: Hypertrophy depends only on volume / frequency / intensity / progression, **not timing**.

### 4. Chronotype moderation

- **Early chronotypes**: evening training → more awakenings + insomnia risk
- **Late chronotypes**: evening training may even improve sleep
- Individual differences require 1-2 weeks of trial to confirm

## Scheduling decision tree

Coach-planner application:

```
Time user gets home = X, target lights-out = Y
→ Compute earliest possible training end time = X + 15min (changing/prep) + training duration
→ Compare gap to lights-out:
   - ≥ 4h → any intensity is OK
   - 2-4h → resistance OK, high-intensity endurance NOT OK
   - 1-2h → only low-intensity resistance OK
   - < 1h → push training later OR push lights-out later OR switch to morning training
```

**Example**: substitute the profile's home-arrival time, lights-out, and training duration into the
decision tree above; don't assume the user's specific times from this file. If the recovery interval
can't be satisfied, prefer switching to morning training, shortening the session, or confirming whether
lights-out can be pushed back; don't silently override the profile/protocol.

## AM Training Physiology (2025 Research)

### Strength performance: short-term dip, 2-3 week adaptation

- AM strength output is **~2-10% lower** than PM (lower core body temperature, lower neuromuscular
  efficiency, incomplete CNS arousal)
- **Adapts after 2-3 weeks of consistent AM training**, can match or exceed the original PM peak (PMC 2023
  meta-analysis)
- First-week strategy: accept RPE running 0.5-1 higher, don't chase PRs, collect AM baseline data
- Source: [Morning vs Evening Training Meta-analysis](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10198889/)

### AM warmup: minimum 10min (PM can be 6min, AM cannot)

AM-specific risks:
- **Disc hydration is highest** (rehydrated overnight while lying down) → increased stiffness, reduced
  flexion tolerance
- Incomplete CNS arousal → poorer neuromuscular coordination → elevated injury risk

AM warmup protocol (10min):
1. **2min raise**: Jumping jacks ×30 + high knees ×20 (core temp +0.5°C)
2. **3min mobility**: arm circles + cat-cow + scapular push-ups
3. **5min 4-tier ramp**: light→moderate→near-working→feeler (e.g. 8kg×8 → 12kg×5 → 14kg×3 → working weight×2)

### Cold exposure & mTOR signaling

- Immediate post-workout cold water immersion may suppress mTOR signaling, **reducing hypertrophy by
  ~10-15%** (Roberts et al. 2015)
- Scheduling rule: **use room-temperature showers on training days** (or cold exposure <1min); save cold
  showers for rest days

### Fasted / semi-fasted AM training

- **2025 meta-analysis**: fasted vs fed training shows **no significant difference** in muscle mass,
  hypertrophy, or strength gain
- Key condition: protein intake within 1-2h post-workout
- AM practical approach: a fast carb (banana) to stabilize blood sugar + half a scoop of whey to fill the
  amino acid pool is enough — no need for a full meal
- Source: [Fasted vs Fed Resistance Training Meta-analysis](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1360859225003043)

### Carb timeline: today's carbs fuel tomorrow's training

- Muscle glycogen is replenished from carbs within 4-6h, and barely depletes during sleep (liver glycogen
  drops ~50%, muscle glycogen stays roughly unchanged)
- Fuel for AM training = **the carbs eaten throughout the previous day**, not what's eaten that morning
- A pre-training banana only tops up liver glycogen and stabilizes blood sugar — the real muscle fuel is
  already there
- **Scheduling implication**: AM trainers still need adequate carbs at dinner the previous day (including
  rest days)
