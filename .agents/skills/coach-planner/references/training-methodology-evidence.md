# Training Methodology — Evidence-Based Reference

Evidence base for training methodology (**not timing-related**; for timing questions see
`training-timing-evidence.md`).

Summarizes common training beliefs debunked or substantially revised in 2023-2026. Coach-planner
should follow the conclusions here when scheduling training — **don't parrot outdated folk wisdom**.

## Debunked / revised common claims (TL;DR)

| # | Old claim | New evidence | Practical implication |
|---|--------|--------|----------|
| 1 | Single-meal protein caps at 40-50g | Trommelen 2023: a single 100g dose sustains MPS stimulation for >12h, no upper limit | No need to spread protein across 4-5 meals; total intake takes priority |
| 2 | 30-60min post-workout Anabolic Window | Actual window is ≥5h; total daily intake dominates | Protein within 1-2h post-workout is enough |
| 3 | Must train to failure to build muscle | 0-3 RIR is equally effective as training to failure for hypertrophy | Leave 1-2 RIR, protect CNS and joints |
| 4 | More DOMS = more effective training | DOMS has no correlation with hypertrophy | Don't chase "muscle soreness" as a goal |
| 5 | High frequency (2x/week/muscle) is required | 1x vs 2x is equivalent at equal volume | 1x/week/muscle is enough; frequency should follow recovery |
| 6 | Weight must be heavy (80%+1RM) to build muscle | 30-85% 1RM produces equivalent hypertrophy near failure | Moderate dumbbell loads at home can still build muscle |
| 7 | Static stretching required before training | >60s static stretch briefly reduces strength ~21%; doesn't prevent injury | Use dynamic warmup instead |
| 8 | Slow tempo / high TUT builds more muscle | 2-8s per rep is equivalent near failure; >8s is actually worse | Normal tempo is fine, no need to deliberately slow down |
| 9 | Ice bath / cold water immersion right after training aids recovery | Post-workout cold water immersion reduces hypertrophy signaling, **chronically blunts muscle gain** | Avoid cold water exposure on training days; save it for rest days or endurance days |
| 10 | Running / cardio "kills gains" | Moderate cardio doesn't impair hypertrophy; only blunts power output | No need to cut Z2 / walking to build muscle |
| 11 | Deload required every 4-8 weeks | 6 weeks of continuous training vs including a deload shows no difference in hypertrophy/strength | Use autoregulation: trigger on HRV/RPE, don't preschedule |
| 12 | Creatine requires a loading phase | 3-5g/day for 28 days reaches the same saturation as loading | No loading week needed; go straight to 5g maintenance |
| 13 | BCAA supplementation alone is useful | When total protein is sufficient, BCAA's marginal benefit ≈ 0; whey/food already contains all EAAs | Don't buy BCAA; spend it on more whey or creatine instead |
| 14 | Must eat before training (fasted harms muscle) | 2025 meta-analysis: fasted vs fed training shows no difference in hypertrophy | AM fasted: half a scoop of whey + a banana is enough |
| 15 | Pre-sleep casein of 30-40g is essential | Trommelen 2023 + no significant benefit when total intake is sufficient | Skip it by default; fold it into a bigger dinner |

## Detailed Evidence

### 1. Training to Failure — unnecessary

**Core finding (Robinson et al. 2024 / Pelland et al. 2024 meta-regression)**:

- Training within **2-3 RIR (Reps in Reserve)** produces nearly identical hypertrophy to training to failure
- Training to failure **significantly increases** CNS fatigue + DOMS + injury risk, for limited extra gain
- **Key exception**: single-joint small muscle groups (curls, lateral raises) still show a small benefit from
  failure

**Practical implication (Coach-Planner)**:
- Compound movements (squat / deadlift / press): **cap at 2 RIR**, never grind to a missed rep
- Isolation movements: 1-0 RIR is acceptable, but only on the last set
- The user's home dumbbell training is compound-focused → apply 1-2 RIR across the board

Sources:
- [Pelland et al. 2024 — Proximity-to-failure dose-response (SportRxiv)](https://sportrxiv.org/index.php/server/preprint/view/295)
- [Robinson et al. 2024 — RIR vs failure 8-week RCT](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/02640414.2024.2321021)
- [Effects to failure systematic review (PubMed 2021)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33497853/)

### 2. DOMS (delayed onset muscle soreness) — not a hypertrophy indicator

- DOMS has **poor correlation** with actual muscle damage, CK elevation, or MPS response (Nosaka 2002 + 2024
  reviews)
- Empirically: DOMS drops sharply after 1-2 weeks of adaptation, **while hypertrophy rate stays the same or
  even increases**
- "No soreness today = didn't train hard enough" is incorrect feedback

**Practical**: Use RPE / weight progression / girth as indicators, **don't use DOMS as a measure**

Source: [DOMS as poor damage marker (PubMed)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12453160/)

### 3. Frequency vs Volume

**Schoenfeld 2024 meta-regression (67 studies, 2,058 subjects)**:
- Volume has a strong dose-response relationship with hypertrophy (diminishing returns but positive up to
  ~20-25 sets/week/muscle)
- **Frequency has almost no effect on hypertrophy at equal volume**
- Frequency only offers a marginal benefit for **strength** (higher frequency favors neural adaptation)

**Practical**:
- 1x/week/muscle at high volume ≈ 2-3x/week/muscle at low volume (for hypertrophy)
- User trains at home with dumbbells and has tight workday recovery time → 1-2x/week/muscle is fine
- Don't worry about "not enough frequency"

Source: [Schoenfeld et al. 2024 — Volume & Frequency Dose-Response](https://sportrxiv.org/index.php/server/preprint/view/460)

### 4. Range of Motion

**Core finding (2024-2025 meta + RCTs)**:
- Full ROM > Partial ROM (traditional view), but the advantage is small (SMD 0.05-0.2, trivial)
- **Lengthened Partials** (partial range at the lengthened muscle position, e.g. bottom 1/3 of a squat) are
  **equivalent** to Full ROM, sometimes slightly favored
- Partial range at the shortened position (top lockout) performs **worst**

**Practical**:
- Default to Full ROM
- Add Lengthened Partials as a variation later or when hitting a plateau (e.g. deficit deadlift bottom /
  deeper Bulgarian split squat)

Sources:
- [Lengthened partials = Full ROM (PeerJ 2024)](https://peerj.com/articles/18904/)
- [Partial vs Full ROM meta-analysis](https://journal.iusca.org/index.php/Journal/article/view/182)

### 5. Load vs Reps (heavy vs light)

**Schoenfeld 2017 + follow-up 2024 RCTs**:
- At equivalent proximity to failure, **hypertrophy is equivalent across 30%-85% 1RM**
- 60-day head-to-head comparison (3-6 reps to failure vs 15-30 reps to failure): **DEXA / MRI / girth all
  equivalent**
- Strength gains **still favor heavier loading** (strong specificity)

**Practical**:
- User's home dumbbells (DB ≤ ~30kg): 8-15 reps/set is **fully sufficient** for hypertrophy
- Don't use "dumbbells are too light to build muscle" as an excuse to skip home training

Source: [Loading Recommendations (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7927075/)

### 6. Pre-Workout Static Stretching

**2024 meta-analysis (83 studies, ES = -0.21)**:
- A single stretch **>60s** acutely reduces strength output by ~21%
- Stretches <60s: strength impact is **negligible**
- Chronic stretching habits **don't prevent injury** (no supporting evidence)

**Practical**:
- Pre-training taboo: single-muscle stretches >60s
- Use dynamic warmup instead (already written into the AM warmup protocol in training-timing-evidence.md)
- Save static stretching for post-workout cooldown or pre-sleep mobility work

Source: [Stretch-induced force deficit meta (2024)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11336295/)

### 7. Tempo / Time Under Tension

**Core finding**:
- **2-8s/rep produces equivalent hypertrophy** (meta-analysis)
- >8s/rep is actually **worse** than normal tempo (because weight/reps are forced down)
- Slow eccentric + fast concentric is the most practical configuration

**Practical**:
- Default tempo: **2-1-1-0** (2s eccentric / 1s pause at bottom / 1s concentric / 0s at top)
- Don't deliberately slow down reps to "increase TUT"

Source: [Eccentric tempo meta-analysis 2024](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/02640414.2025.2535198)

### 8. Cold Water Immersion / Ice Baths — avoid post-workout

**Piñero et al. 2024 meta-analysis (European J Sport Science)**:
- Post-workout CWI reduces MPS for 5h; chronically **blocks muscle fiber hypertrophy** (strength effect)
  while preserving **maximal strength**
- Historical evidence (Roberts 2015 / Fyfe 2019) consistently replicates this

**Practical (already reflected in training-timing-evidence.md)**:
- **No** cold showers / ice baths / cold exposure >1min on training days
- Save cold exposure for rest days OR endurance days (CWI doesn't affect endurance adaptation)
- Room-temperature water is fine for the user at home

Source: [Piñero et al. 2024 — Throwing cold water on muscle growth](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/ejsc.12074)

### 9. Concurrent Training (endurance + strength)

**2024 meta-analysis (by sex & training status)**:
- Concurrent training **barely interferes** with hypertrophy
- Only blunts **power output** (jump / sprint)
- Interference mainly occurs in **males + high-volume endurance work (>3x/week + long duration)** combined
  in the same session

**Practical**:
- The user's running / Z2 won't "kill gains"
- Scheduling rule: do cardio **before** strength training (or on a different day)
- Endurance sessions **<3x/week** are entirely safe

Source: [Concurrent Training Meta 2024 (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10933151/)

### 10. Deload Week

**Coleman et al. 2024 RCT**:
- 6 weeks of continuous training vs including 1 deload week: **no significant difference** in
  hypertrophy/strength
- Strength sport periodization needs deloads; hypertrophy training doesn't require them

**Practical (autoregulation beats a fixed schedule)**:
- Trigger conditions (any one): HRV <70% of baseline for 7 consecutive days / training RPE consistently >9 /
  subjective burnout / joint pain ≥3 days
- Deload format: volume ×50% (not 0% — complete rest actually makes restarting harder)
- **Don't preschedule "deload every 4-6 weeks"** — trigger based on data

Sources:
- [Coleman et al. 2024 — Deload week study (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10809978/)
- [Practical deloading recommendations (Bell)](https://shura.shu.ac.uk/35313/3/Bell-APracticalApproach(AM).pdf)

### 11. Creatine Loading

- 5g/day ×28 days = same saturation level as a 20g loading dose ×7 days
- Loading only helps see results **3 weeks earlier**; no difference to long-term gains
- The user is already stable at 5g/day → skipping loading is entirely correct

Source: [Creatine dose-response (ScienceDirect 2024)](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2950273X24000249)

### 12. BCAA / isolated leucine

- Isolated BCAA supplementation biases toward triggering mTOR but lacks other EAAs → **MPS doesn't actually
  increase** (Wolfe 2017)
- When total protein ≥1.6g/kg and sources contain ≥3g leucine/meal, BCAA's marginal benefit = 0
- The user already hits protein targets daily → BCAA is **pure waste**

Source: [Wolfe 2017 BCAA review (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6718193/)

## Coach-Planner Application Rules

When scheduling training / giving advice, **do not write** the following dogma:
- ❌ "Training to failure is the only effective way"
- ❌ "60-second static stretch warmup"
- ❌ "Longer TUT is always better, go slower"
- ❌ "30-minute golden window post-workout"
- ❌ "Deload every 6 weeks, mandatory"
- ❌ "Frequency below 2x/week won't build muscle"
- ❌ "Ice for 10 minutes after training to aid recovery"

**Should write**:
- ✅ 1-2 RIR, last set can push to 0 RIR
- ✅ 5-10min dynamic warmup (see AM warmup protocol)
- ✅ Tempo 2-1-1-0
- ✅ 30g+ protein within 1-2h post-workout is enough
- ✅ Trigger deload on HRV/RPE, don't preschedule
- ✅ 1-2x/week/muscle at high volume is equivalent to high frequency at low volume
- ✅ Avoid cold water exposure on training days
